About

Hello!

I'm currently a Wiener-Anspach Visiting Doctoral Fellow at the Université libre de Bruxelles, working with Sandrine Detandt in the Faculty of Psychology. My research at the ULB concerns service user perspectives on the diagnosis of gender dysphoria. If you'd like to take part in my research, send me an email!

When I've finished my visiting fellowship at the ULB, I'll return to Cambridge to finish writing up my PhD thesis. My PhD is supervised by Professor Clare Chambers and Dr Jessie Munton and financially supported by a Levy-Plumb Doctoral Scholarship from the Arts and Humanities Research Council and Christ's College, Cambridge.

Before my PhD, I studied Mathematics and Philosophy at Merton College, Oxford. After that, I completed an MPhil in Philosophy at Darwin College, Cambridge, funded by the Cambridge Trust.

My doctoral research combines philosophy of medicine and medical ethics with social history and political philosophy. Mostly, I work on the diagnosis of gender dypshoria and the concept of gender identity. At the moment, I'm working on a genealogy of gender identity and gender dysphoria, under contract with Cambridge Elements in Psychology and Culture. I've recently published a philosophical account of what a gender identity is and what it means to have one, and I have been working on a paper exploring how psychiatrists should determine whether controversial and emerging diagnoses are worth their salt. More broadly, I’m interested in the metaphysics of social identities – in how our sense of self is negotiated in dialogue with others, using resources made available by our social context. In a different area of philosophy, I've also published on political disobedience.

I have supervised various undergraduate Philosophy papers at Cambridge spanning technical, theoretical, and applied philosophy. In 2025/6, I wrote and delivered a series of four lectures for the Part II (third year) Political Philosophy paper. While I'm at the ULB, I'm planning to give a guest lecture for the Faculty of Psychology as part of the 'Sexualités, genre et psycho(patho)logie' course.

Outside of research and teaching, I sometimes work as a freelance mezzo-soprano. I was the first woman to be a lay clerk with the Choir of St John's College, Cambridge, where I sang for two years, and before that I sang with the choir of Merton College, Oxford. I'm a long-distance runner, an enthusiastic cyclist, and a fan of cold-water swimming at the Cambridge lido. Putting these together, I'm an aspiring triathlete. I also have a long-term goal of bagging all of the Scottish Munros and Lake District Wainwrights.
